How AI Brains Actually Work

Ever wondered how your phone recognizes your face, or how TikTok knows exactly what video you'll like? Let’s crack open the hood of Artificial Neural Networks.

1 From Biological Brains to Code

When you see a dog in real life, your brain instantly knows it's a dog. You don't sit down and measure its tail length or calculate its fur percentage—your brain's network of roughly 86 billion biological neurons processes all that visual data in a fraction of a second.

For decades, traditional computer code couldn't do this. Regular code relies on strict rules like if/else statements. But how do you write an if/else statement for every possible picture of a dog ever taken? It's impossible!

Enter Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs):

Instead of giving a computer strict rules, we build a mathematical structure inspired by biological brains and let it learn from examples.

2 The Input Layer: Translating the World into Numbers

Computers don't see colors, hear sounds, or read words—they only understand numbers. The Input Layer acts as the digital translator that converts real-world items into mathematical arrays called tensors.

🖼️ Photos

Converted to pixel grid numbers ($0$ to $255$ brightness values).

📝 Text

Words turned into lists of numbers called embeddings.

🎵 Audio

Sound waves broken down into frequency numbers over time.

Why Scaling Math Matters (Data Normalization)

Imagine predicting if someone gets onto a varsity team using two inputs: Height in inches ($60$ to $80$) and Annual Income ($\$20,000$ to $\$500,000$).

Because income numbers are giant, the computer would mistakenly assume income is thousands of times more important than height! To fix this, we normalize inputs to a small scale, usually between $0.0$ and $1.0$:

$$x_{\text{scaled}} = \frac{x - x_{\min}}{x_{\max} - x_{\min}}$$

3 Hidden Layers: The Detective Work

Squeezed between the input and output are the Hidden Layers. They're called "hidden" simply because we don't directly feed them data or directly read their final answer—they operate behind the scenes.

Think of hidden layers as a team of detectives analyzing an image in stages:

Layer 1 Detects simple raw lines, diagonal edges, and color changes.
Layer 2 Combines lines into shapes, corners, circles, and textures.
Layer 3+ Combines shapes into complex objects like eyes, snout shapes, or wheel rims.

What Happens Inside Each Artificial Neuron?

Each neuron performs two quick math steps:

  1. Multiply & Add (Weights and Biases): It multiplies every incoming signal by a weight ($w$), adds them together, and adds a small baseline threshold called a bias ($b$).
    $$z = (w_1 \cdot x_1) + (w_2 \cdot x_2) + \dots + b$$
  2. Fire or Stay Quiet (Activation): It passes $z$ into an activation function to decide how strongly to send the signal to the next layer.

4 Activation Functions: The Spark of Non-Linearity

Why do we need activation functions? Because without them, the entire neural network would just be doing basic linear math (drawing straight lines).

Real-world data isn't straight—it's messy, curved, and complex. Activation functions bend the math so the AI can learn complex, curved boundaries.

ReLU

$f(z) = \max(0, z)$

The Light Switch: If the signal is negative, output $0$. If positive, keep it as is. Super fast and popular!

Sigmoid

$\sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z}}$

The Squisher: Squishes any number into a nice smooth percentage between $0.0$ ($0\%$) and $1.0$ ($100\%$).

GELU

$z \cdot \Phi(z)$

The Modern Standard: A smooth version of ReLU used in cutting-edge LLMs like ChatGPT and Claude!

Function Output Range Best Used For
ReLU $[0, \infty)$ Hidden layers in computer vision
Sigmoid $(0, 1)$ Yes/No binary predictions
GELU $\approx (-0.17, \infty)$ Modern AI Transformers & Text Models

5 Putting It All Together: Cat vs. Dog

Let's trace how a photo travels through the whole system:

Step 1
Input Layer

Feed in $28 \times 28$ pixels ($784$ total numbers scaled between $0.0$ and $1.0$).

Step 2
Hidden Layer 1

Calculates linear weights and applies ReLU to detect sharp edges.

Step 3
Hidden Layer 2

Combines edges into floppy ears, whiskers, and muzzle shapes.

Step 4
Output Layer

Outputs final odds:
Dog: 94% | Cat: 6%

Quick Takeaways to Remember 🧠

  • ✦ Input Layer: Converts real-world images, text, or audio into standardized numbers.
  • ✦ Hidden Layers: The pattern detectors that find simple features first, then combine them into complex concepts.
  • ✦ Activation Functions: The non-linear "switches" that give the network power to handle complex, curved real-world problems.
